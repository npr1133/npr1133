Introduction of the Republic of China(Taiwan)

Taiwan—officially known as the Republic of China (ROC)—is an island country situated in the northwestern waters of the Pacific Ocean. It shares maritime borders with the People's Republic of China to the west, Japan to the northeast, and the Philippines to the south. The country's geography is characterised by distinct terrain: rugged mountain chains cover the eastern two-thirds, while plains dominate the western third.

Introduction of the cities of Taiwan

  • Taipei
    The vibrant capital city of Taiwan, serving as the political, economic, and cultural epicentre. It is famous for the iconic Taipei 101 skyscraper and bustling night markets.
  • New Taipei
    The most populous city and the largest city proper in Taiwan, completely surrounding the capital. It blends massive urban districts with scenic coastal areas and historic mountain towns like Jiufen.
  • Kaohsiung
    The primary harbour and maritime hub of the south. Known for its massive shipping ports, artistic pier transformations, and the iconic Love River.
  • Keelung
    A rainy, historical port city situated on the northern coast. It is widely celebrated across the island for its historic fortresses and the famous Miaokou Night Market.
  • Tainan
    The oldest city in Taiwan and its former historic capital. It is deeply treasured as the heart of traditional culture, featuring centuries-old temples and famous street food.
  • Taoyuan
    The main international gateway to the island, housing Taiwan's primary international airport. It is a rapidly growing industrial, technology, and economic hub.
