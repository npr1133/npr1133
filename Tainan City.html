Let's explore the city of Tainan

Welcome to Tainan

Tainan City is the oldest city in Taiwan and serves as the island's ultimate cultural, historic, and culinary capital. Established as a Dutch trading port in the 17th century and later serving as the island's capital for over 200 years under imperial rule, the city is a living museum packed with ancient temples, historic forts, and traditional wooden townhouses. It is internationally celebrated for its legendary street food heritage, boasting unique regional specialities like coffin bread, eel noodles, and milkfish belly found across vibrant night markets. Alongside landmarks like Chihkan Tower and Anping Old Fort, Tainan features a thriving modern arts scene with creatively repurposed warehouses and trendy boutique alleyways.

Anping old fort

Anping Old Fort is a historic fortress located in the coastal Anping District of Tainan City. Originally built by the Dutch East India Company between 1624 and 1634 as Fort Zeelandia, it served as the historic international trading hub and administrative centre for western Taiwan. Today, visitors can explore the red-brick remnants of the ancient outer fortress wall, climb a distinct white observation tower built during the 20th century, and wander through an onsite museum detailing the colonial conflicts between European, Chinese, and Japanese forces.

Anping tree house

Anping Tree House is a surreal and captivating historic site located directly behind the former Tait & Co. Merchant House in Tainan City. Originally built in the late 19th century as a trade warehouse for coal and salt, the brick structure was abandoned after the local salt industry declined following the mid-20th century.
